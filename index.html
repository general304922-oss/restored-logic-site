A corporate laptop is written off years before it stops working. We buy those machines, put right what is missing, and send them back to work.

What we do

Business laptops leave their first life in bulk — lease returns, office refreshes, recycler pallets. Most are three to six years old, built to a far higher standard than consumer machines, and still have years of service in them.

A missing drive, a flat battery or a wiped operating system is enough to move one from an asset to a write-off. None of those are the machine failing. They are just the reasons it stopped being someone's problem.

How it works

  1. AssessmentEvery unit is evaluated against current market data before purchase, so we know what a machine is worth and what it will cost to make it whole.
  2. RestorationDrives, memory, batteries and power supplies replaced as needed. Storage is wiped, a clean licensed operating system installed, and the hardware tested before anything is listed.
  3. Honest listingSpecifications are stated accurately and completely. Buyers should know exactly what is arriving, including anything we could not put right.

Why used

Most of a computer's footprint is spent before it is ever switched on. Keeping one in service another few years is the largest single reduction available.

A well-specified machine from 2019 also outperforms most new budget hardware, at a fraction of the price. The environmental case and the practical one point the same way.
